# Introduction

*Artificial Intelligence* (or *AI*) is a fast-evolving field of technology that lets computers simulate human functions, such as learning and problem-solving. A subset of AI that's been gaining traction recently is *generative AI*. This branch of AI specializes in creating new content, be it text, images, audio, or videos. <br />
How is this AI able to be so creative? Generative AI is trained using vast amounts of data, for example, billions of sentence, millions of images, or years of audio. By analyzing these inputs, the AI identifies patterns and rules and then uses these to generate new, original content in a similar style or format. <br />
There are many real-world examples of generative AI in use today, including ChatGPT, which can generate text, and Dall-E, which can create artwork. <br />

## Text-Based Generative AI

Some of the greatest possibilities open up when AI meets text. Text-based generative AI has the ability to produce human-like writing, making it a powerful tool for many applications. <br />
A popular example of this technology is ChatGPT, an AI model trained on millions of web pages. This training is what gives ChatGPT its knowledge of modern topics and the ability to give realistic output. <br />
To be more specific, it can create written content, such as articles or blog posts, making content writers more productive and allowing domain experts to communicate their ideas more easily. These generative AIs can also automate common interactions, such as email responses or chatbot conversations, providing more expedient and accurate customer service. Or assist in routine tasks like scheduling meetings or sending reminders, effectively acting as a virtual assistant. <br />
One surprising application of text-based AI is personalized tutoring. An AI can be programmed to answer specific questions on content, providing a unique learning experience tailored to each individual. Or if the user wants to read content originally from another language, the AI can translate the text as well. <br />
For business applications, generative text AIs have made progress in data reporting and analyzing and summarizing data. They have also found use in programming, where they have been used to generate code for simple tasks or edit existing code to reduce errors. <br />

## Image-Based Generative AI

Some of the most impressive results from generative AIs come from those that generate images. With only a text description, masterpieces can easily be generated, even on most home computers. <br />
A remarkable example of this technology is Dall-E. It is trained using millions of images from the internet, giving it the capability to generate images about any topic and imitating the art style of any era. <br />
So, what are the applications of image-based generative AI? Besides creating completely new images, this AI can also modify existing images based on inputted templates. This revolutionizes the graphic design industry, allowing those with less artistic talent to more easily convey their ideas and designs. <br />
AI can also be used to customize designs in sectors like clothing or architecture before beginning manufacturing or construction, saving resources and improving customer satisfaction. It can further be used to help train workers. For example, images for specific diagnoses can be generated to train medical professionals about rare diseases or surgeries, without violating a patient's privacy.

## Audio-Based Generative AI

The applications for audio-based generative AI are truly diverse. Whether creating new voices, original songs, or sounds of animals in a rainforest, there are many possibilities. <br />
For music creation, audio-based generative AI can use a simple text description to create entirely new compositions or finish an existing song. This could lead to more innovative sounds as musicians are able to more easily move past traditional instruments. <br />
Another significant application is text-to-speech conversion. That AI capability can convert written text into spoken words that carry natural-sounding inflection and tonality, which can be instrumental in accessibility tools or interactive customer service. <br />
A related, but more advanced application of audio-based AI is voice cloning, where an AI system can mimic a specific person's voice for users such as dubbing films or providing voiceovers. <br />
AI can also be used in audio restoration and enhancement, improving the quality of old or noisy recordings. This can have significant applications in preserving historical records or enhancing sound quality in existing media.

## Video-Based Generative AI

At the intersection of images and audio is video-based generative AI. <br />
Many of the applications for this type of AI are more advanced combinations of the previous image and audio examples. For example, video-based generative AI can replace the appearance of one person with the likeness of another in a video, a technique know as "facial replacement" or "deepfake". This capability can fundamentally change the film and advertising industry, possibly leading to more personalized content generated on demand. <br />
This type of AI is also great for boosting efficiency and creativity in production by automating certain video editing and special effects tasks. Along with this, AI can also generate synthetic video data for use in training autonomous vehicles, security teams, and medical professionals. <br />
Video-based generative AI can be a great help to existing video as well. For videos such as sporting events, AI can create analytic overlays providing real-time insights, enhancing the viewer experience. AI can also be used to enhance low-quality or damaged videos, which was not previously possible.

## Risks and Limitations of Using Generative AI

While generative AI is a very promising technology, it's crucial to understand its limitations and potential for misuse. Each form of generative AI has its own unique set of challenges and risks. <br />
Text-based generative AI, for example, may produce incorrect or nonsensical responses due to its focus on identifying patterns in text, not necessarily identifying facts. There is also a lack of access to current data, leaving some output to be outdated. <br />
Audio-based generative AI raises a variety of legal issues. What should the licensing and copyright look like for training data or the generated output? And when this audio is generated, how can its misuse be prevented, for example in cases of impersonation? Even for more just causes of audio-based generative AI, if used for customer support, the reality of talking to a machine could feel impersonal and lead to frustrating errors. <br />
Similar issues exist in video-based generative AI as well. Video has traditionally been viewed as a higher standard for more truthful content, but with AI being able to create deceptive content, unethically using someone's likeness without their consent, that reputation may become tarnished. <br />
Accross all the types of generative AIs there are also more general issues. Data privacy is a concern as users could upload sensitive information to AI platforms without the consent of others. There's also a risk of information overload, with AIs being able to rapidly produce and distribute vast amounts of content, which can be used for fraudulent reviews, false narratives, or comment spamming.

## Ethics of Using Generative AI

With this incredible potential, generative AI also comes with significant ethical questions. <br />
One of the main ethical concerns is the potential for massive job loss due to AI automation. Some news outlets have already laid off their staff because of the success they had with generative AI. Generative image and video AI are also making visuals easier to create. As this happens, fewer graphics designers and special effects will be needed to produce the same amount of content, likely leading to less hiring of these positions in the future. <br />
How will society handle such a sudden, unprecedented shift in labor? Will wealth be distributed differently if most people no longer work due to automation? <br />
Another contentious issue is the use of AI to generate content similar to that used for training. Do the owners of the original content have any ownership over the generated content? And who can claim copyright over content generated by AI if, for example, it were to write a sequel to a popular novel series? These questions are currently being discussed in courtrooms because of lawsuits by famous authors. The results of this litigation will likely be landmark cases that forever change copyright law, defining how AI can be used on any type of data. <br />
The content generated by AI can also be especially devastating if the content is used to mislead. Most social media platforms today have strict rules banning this type of content because of the real harm that can result from believing in misinformation. However, moderating content is hard, and in the crusade to remove content that appears AI-generated, writings and artwork created by real people can mistakenly be targeted too.

## AI vs. Generative AI

Since its creation, Artificial Intelligence (AI) has continually evolved, branching into many specialties and applications. Of all these branches, Generative AI stands out with its stunning output. In this section, we will define these two fields, clarify misconceptions, and highlight their differences.

### What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence is a fascinating and evolving branch of computer science that focuses on creating systems capable of performing tasks that historically required human intelligence. These tasks encompass a wide range, such as recognizing speech, understanding human languages, making decisions, and identifying patterns or objects in images. AI combines elements from various fields, including computer science, cognitive science, linguistics, and psychology, creating a rich interdisciplinary area of study.

### Common Applications of AI

The applications of AI are diverse and impactful across many fields. In healthcare, AI can assist in diagnosing diseases, personalizing treatment plans, and even in drug discovery. In the finance industry, AI is often used for fraud detection, algorithmic trading, and personalized financial advice. The automotive industry has seen leaps in AI with the development of autonomous vehicles. In retail, AI can enhance customer experience through personalized recommendations and inventory management. Additionally, AI plays a signifcant role in security, with applications in surveillance, cybersecurity, and anomaly detection.

### What is Generative AI?

Generative AI, a subset of artificial intelligence, marks a significant leap in the capabilities of machines, enabling them to not just make decisions or predictions but also to create and generate new, original content. This content can be span various forms, including text, images, videos, music, and even code. The core of Generative AI is its ability to take existing data, learn from it, and then use that learned information to generate new yet, similar data that didn't previously exist.

### Key Characteristics of Generative AI

<ol>
  <li><strong>Creativity and Originality</strong>: Generative AI systems can produce original content, often indistinguishable from human-created content. This includes everything from artwork to text to music.</li>
  <li><strong>Adaptability</strong>: These systems can be trained on various data sets, enabling them to generate content in numerous domains, be it writing in the style of a specific author or creating art in the vein of a particular genre.</li>
  <li><strong>Self-Improvement</strong>: Through techniques like reinforcement learning and the adversarial process seen in GANs, Generative AI models can continuously improve their output by learning from past successes and failures.</li>
</ol>

### Differences Between AI and Generative AI

#### Objectives: Decision-making vs. Creation

AI primarily focused on decision-making, analysis, and problem-solving. It's about making sense of data, extracting patterns, and applying these insights to make informed decisions. For instance, AI in a navigation app analyzes traffic data to recommend the fastest route. <br />
Generative AI, on the other hand, is centered around creation and innovation. It goes beyong analysis to generate new, original outputs that didn't previously exist. For example, a Generative AI model can compose a piece of music in a certain style or generate realistic images of people who don't exist.

#### Data Processing Approaches

AI systems interpret and act upon data. They are often fed large amounts of data and use algorithms to find patterns and solutions within this dataset. This process can involve classification, regression, or clustering tasks. <br />
Generative AI models use data as a learning foundation to understand a particular style, pattern, or structure, and then use that understanding to create new, similar data. They don't just find patterns in data; they use these patterns as a blueprint for generation.

#### Different Applications in Various Fields

<ul>
  <li>In <strong>healthcare</strong>, traditional AI might be used for analyzing medical images to detect diseases, while Generative AI could be employed to create synthetic medical images for training AI systems without compromising patient privacy.</li>
  <li>In the <strong>finance sector</strong>, AI is used for predictive analysis, like forecasting stock trends, whereas Generative AI could potentially create realistic financial scenarios for stress testing or training purposes.</li>
  <li>In <strong>art and entertainment</strong>, while AI might recommend movies or music based on user preferences, Generative AI can create new artworks or music pieces.</li>
</ul>

### Misconceptions about AI and Generative AI

#### Myth: All AI Can Create New Content

**Reality**: A common misconception is that all AI systems can generate new, original content. In reality, most AI systems are designed for specific tasks like pattern recognition, predictive analysis, or decision-making. Generative AI, a specialized subset of AI, is what focuses on creating new content.

#### Misunderstanding: AI Has Human-Like Understanding

**Clarification**: Another widespread myth is that AI systems possess a human-like understanding or consciousness. AI, including Generative AI, operates based on data and algorithms and lacks true comprehension or consciousness. While AI can mimic certain aspects of human intelligence or creativity, it does so within a narrowly defined framework set by its programming and training.

#### Conflation of AI's Decision-Making with Creativity

**Explanation**: Often, AI's ability to make decisions or predictions is conflated with creativity. However, decision-making AI is based on analyzing existing data and following predetermined algorithms, whereas creativity, as exhibited by Generative AI, involves creating something new and original.

#### Overestimating AI Capabilities

**Insight**: There is a tendency to overestimate what AI and Generative AI can achieve. While these technologies have made significant strides, they are not infallible and have limitations in terms of understanding context, dealing with novel situations, and ethical decision-making.
